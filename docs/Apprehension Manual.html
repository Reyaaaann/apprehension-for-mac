1. Quick start

  1. Put it on a sample. In FL Studio, load Apprehension in an effect slot on the mixer track that holds your sample (open an empty slot's menu and pick Apprehension from the plugin list; run Options > Manage plugins > Find installed plugins first if you do not see it). Works the same in FL Studio for Windows and for Mac.
  2. Pick a preset. Click the preset name at the top (or the arrows) and choose one of the 20 sample-flipping presets. Press play. That is all you need to get a sound.
  3. Adjust to taste. Hover over any control for a one-line explanation. Turn Mix to set how much reversed sound you hear, and Overall Pitch to retune the whole sample.

Handy things to know: double-click a knob to reset it · click the number under a knob to type a value · drag a corner of the window to resize it · hover over anything to see what it does.

2. How it works

Apprehension constantly records the sound that goes into it. Every so often it plays the last stretch of that recording backwards. Each stretch is called a chunk, and the Division (or Time) knob sets how long it is. Your original sound still passes straight through, and the Mix knob blends the two.

The reversed sound then travels through this chain, in order:

  1. Pitch (Reverse Pitch), Attack, Cutoff/Release, Smooth - shape each chunk.
  2. Diffusion - softens the sound.
  3. Low Cut and High Cut filters - the point where Feedback is taken from, so repeats are filtered too.
  4. Chorus, then Flangus, then Reverb.
  5. Ducking, then Width.

After that it is mixed with your original sound (Mix), set to the Output level, and optionally run through Signature Mode's auto-chop, then Overall Pitch, then Soft Clip. Everything in this last group affects everything you hear, not only the reversed layer.

Latency: Overall Pitch adds a fixed 25 ms delay (always, so nothing shifts when you move the knob). The plugin tells your DAW, and FL Studio compensates automatically, so everything stays in time.

Time - the reverse engine

ControlRange / optionsDefaultWhat it does
SyncOn / OffOnChooses how the length of each reversed chunk is set. On = a musical note length (Division) that follows your project's tempo. Off = a fixed time in milliseconds (Time). If your host does not send a tempo, Sync falls back to Time.
Time AlignOn / OffOffWithout Time Align, the reversed chunks start whenever you press play and slowly drift against the beat (each chunk overlaps the next a little). With Time Align on, every chunk starts exactly on a grid line of your project, and the reversed sound mirrors around each beat: something that was 100 ms before a beat comes back 100 ms after it, and a hit that was exactly on a beat comes back exactly on a beat. It needs the project to be playing (it uses the host's beat position). Keep Smooth low if you want hits to stay sharp.
Division1/16, 1/8T, 1/8, 1/4T, 1/4, 1/2, 1 bar, 2 bars1/4The note length of each reversed chunk when Sync is on. Short values (1/16, 1/8) give rhythmic, stuttery reverse hits. 1/4 and 1/2 are the classic reverse-into-the-beat sound. 1 bar and 2 bars give long, dramatic swells. T means triplet.
Time10 to 4000 ms500 msThe length of each reversed chunk when Sync is off. Very short times (10-60 ms) turn into a gritty, pitched buzz; hundreds of milliseconds give classic reverse echoes.
Attack0 to 2000 ms0 msHow long each reversed chunk takes to fade in. 0 ms starts at full volume. 100-400 ms gives a smooth swell. It is this knob that decides how fast the reversed sound starts.
Cutoff5 to 100 %100 %Controls where each chunk stops. At 100% the whole chunk plays. At 50% only the first half of every chunk plays and then there is silence until the next chunk. Use it to make tight, gated reverse hits.
Release1 to 2000 ms30 msThe fade-out time at the Cutoff point. Short = a clean chop. Long = the chunk tails away gently. You only hear it when Cutoff is below 100%.
Smooth0 to 100 %50 %How much one reversed chunk overlaps and crossfades into the next. Low values keep every chunk sharp and punchy. High values blend the chunks into a continuous wash.

Attack, Cutoff and Release together make the shape of every reversed chunk: Attack = how fast it starts, Cutoff = where it ends, Release = how softly it ends.

Pitch

ControlRange / optionsDefaultWhat it does
Overall Pitch-12 to 12 st0 stShifts the pitch of everything the plugin outputs - both your original sound and the reversed layer - without changing the speed or timing. Use it to retune a sample to your key (the Key Detector tells you what key it will land in). Adds a fixed 25 ms of latency that your DAW compensates automatically. Quality is best within about 5 semitones; the further you go, the more the sound takes on a granular, robotic character.
Reverse Pitch-24 to 24 st0 stShifts only the reversed layer, like speeding up or slowing down a tape (so the chunk is read faster or slower). Combine it with Overall Pitch: the reversed layer's total shift is the two added together. Very high values shorten the longest chunk the plugin can play, because it needs to read further back in time.
Ref Hz20 to 2000 Hz440 HzA reference frequency for the pitch readout. Type a frequency (for example the pitch of the note you care about) and the panel shows what it becomes after the Overall and Reverse pitch changes, in Hz and as a note name, plus the difference in Hz. It does not change the sound.

Key detector (the panel under Pitch). It listens to your incoming sound for a few seconds, works out which musical key it is in (for example "A minor") and shows a bar for each of the 12 notes. Underneath it shows what that key becomes after your Overall Pitch change (and, in small writing, after the Reverse Pitch change too). A fraction of a semitone is shown in cents. It works best on melodic samples with a few seconds of music; drums, noise and very quiet sounds will confuse it, and it shows a confidence percentage so you know how sure it is. It also shows the pitch readout in Hz: the reference frequency you typed, and what it turns into.

Space & Modulation

ControlRange / optionsDefaultWhat it does
Feedback0 to 95 %25 %Sends the reversed sound back into the buffer so it gets reversed again and echoes. 20-40% is a subtle trail, 60-80% builds long swirling repeats. The feedback is soft-limited, so it cannot run away.
Diffusion0 to 100 %20 %Runs the reversed sound through a chain of all-pass filters that smear transients without changing the tone. Higher values soften hard hits into a gentle haze.
Width0 to 200 %100 %Narrows or widens the stereo image of the reversed sound only. 0% collapses it to mono, 100% leaves it as it is, 200% pushes it extra wide.
Chorus0 to 100 %0 %Thickens the reversed sound with a chorus. The Chorus Type knob picks the character; this knob sets how much.
Chorus TypeClassic, Ensemble, Dimension, Vibrato, TapeClassicPicks the kind of chorus. Classic = two voices, the familiar chorus. Ensemble = three slow voices, lush and wide. Dimension = four short voices with almost no obvious movement, just width. Vibrato = pitch wobble only, with no dry sound added. Tape = slow wow plus fast flutter, like an old tape machine.
Flangus0 to 100 %0 %A sweeping comb-filter effect on the reversed sound, in the spirit of FL Studio's Fruity Flangus. The Flangus Type knob picks the character; this knob sets how much.
Flangus TypeFruity, Jet, Metallic, Soft, WideFruityPicks the kind of flange. Fruity = the classic medium sweep. Jet = deep, slow and strong, like a passing plane. Metallic = negative feedback, a hollow ringing tone. Soft = gentle and subtle. Wide = the left and right sides sweep in opposite directions for a huge stereo sweep.

Chorus types: Classic, Ensemble, Dimension, Vibrato, Tape.   Flangus types: Fruity, Jet, Metallic, Soft, Wide. They are described in the table above.

Ducking

ControlRange / optionsDefaultWhat it does
Amount0 to 100 %0 %Turns the reversed sound down so it gets out of the way. 0% = no ducking. 100% = it disappears completely when triggered. What triggers it is set by the Pump button.
Release10 to 1000 ms200 msThe recovery time after each duck. Short = snappy. Long = a slow swell back up. In Pump mode it is limited to one cycle of the chosen division.
Division1/16, 1/8, 1/4, 1/2, 1/11/4In Pump mode, how often the ducking happens: every 1/16, 1/8, 1/4, 1/2 or 1/1 (a whole bar). 1/4 is a pulse on every beat, like sidechain compression from a kick. It follows the host tempo.
PumpOn / OffOffOff = Input ducking: the reversed sound gets quieter whenever your original sound is loud, so the reversed layer fills the gaps. On = Pump: the reversed sound dips on every beat of the chosen Division, whatever you play - the classic sidechain pump.

Reverb

ControlRange / optionsDefaultWhat it does
TypeRoom, Hall, Plate, Chamber, CathedralHallPicks the reverb's space. Room = small and quick. Hall = big and smooth. Plate = dense and bright, like a studio plate. Chamber = medium and warm. Cathedral = huge and slow. Size scales each one.
Mix0 to 100 %0 %The amount of reverb. It is applied to the reversed sound, so the main Mix must be above 0% to hear it. The reverb keeps ringing after the reversed sound stops.
Size0 to 100 %50 %Scales the size of the reverb space. Small = tight and close. Large = spacious and slow. Moving it while sound plays gives a gentle pitch glide in the tail.
Decay0.2 to 15 s2.5 sThe length of the reverb tail (the time it takes to fade by 60 dB). Under 1 s is tight. 2-4 s is natural. 8 s and up is an ambient wash.
Pre-Delay0 to 250 ms20 msA pause before the reverb begins. 20-40 ms keeps the front of the reversed sound clear before the reverb blooms; long pre-delays make a distinct echo-then-wash effect.
Damping0 to 100 %40 %How much the reverb tail darkens as it fades. 0% keeps it bright and glassy. High values make it warm and dull, like a soft room.
Width0 to 200 %100 %The stereo width of the reverb only.
Modulation0 to 100 %20 %Gently modulates the reverb to avoid a metallic ring and add shimmer. Low values are natural; high values make the tail wobble like a chorus.

Filter

ControlRange / optionsDefaultWhat it does
Low Cut20 to 2000 Hz (20 = off)OffA high-pass filter that removes low frequencies from the reversed sound. At its minimum (20 Hz) it is switched off. Use it to keep the reversed layer from muddying the bass.
Low Shape6 dB, 12 dB, 18 dB, 24 dB, 36 dB, 48 dB, 72 dB, 96 dB, Brickwall24 dBThe steepness of the Low Cut. 6 dB per octave is a very gentle tilt. 12 and 24 dB are the usual choices. 36 to 96 dB are very steep, and Brickwall is an almost vertical wall (32nd order) that removes everything below the frequency. Watch the graph - it draws the exact curve.
Low Reso0 to 12 dB0 dBBoosts the frequencies right at the Low Cut corner for a more pronounced, vocal 'filter sweep' sound. 0 dB is neutral. It has no effect on the 6 dB shape.
High Cut70 to 20000 Hz (20000 = off)OffA low-pass filter that removes high frequencies from the reversed sound. At its maximum (20 kHz) it is switched off. It goes all the way down to 70 Hz, so you can turn the reversed layer into a muffled low rumble.
High Shape6 dB, 12 dB, 18 dB, 24 dB, 36 dB, 48 dB, 72 dB, 96 dB, Brickwall24 dBThe steepness of the High Cut, from a gentle 6 dB per octave to a near-vertical Brickwall. The graph shows the exact curve.
High Reso0 to 12 dB0 dBBoosts the frequencies right at the High Cut corner for a sharper, more 'synthy' filter sound. 0 dB is neutral. It has no effect on the 6 dB shape.

The graph shows the real curve made by both filters, from 20 Hz to 20 kHz. Low Cut removes everything below its frequency; High Cut removes everything above. Shapes run 6, 12, 18, 24, 36, 48, 72, 96 dB per octave and Brickwall. The filters act on the reversed sound only (your original sound is not filtered).

Output

ControlRange / optionsDefaultWhat it does
Mix0 to 100 %50 %Blends your original (dry) sound with the reversed (wet) sound. It is an equal-power crossfade, so the loudness stays steady as you turn it. At 100% you hear only the reversed sound - turn on Time Align so it stays on the beat. The reversed sound is what the reverb, chorus, flangus, filters, ducking and width act on, so at 0% those are not heard.
Output-24 to 12 dB0 dBThe overall level leaving the plugin, from -24 dB to +12 dB. Use it to match your sample's loudness after adding effects.
Soft ClipOn / OffOffA gentle limiter-style safety. Anything below about -3 dB passes untouched; louder peaks are rounded off smoothly so the output can never go past 0 dBFS. Handy after adding feedback, chop or pitch effects.

The level meter under the knobs shows the peak output level in dB.

Header

ControlRange / optionsDefaultWhat it does
FreezeOn / OffOffFreeze stops the plugin from recording new audio and keeps playing the last chunk backwards in a loop. Press it at a moment you like to hold a sound; press it again to carry on. Your original sound keeps playing through the Mix as normal.
@Reyaan. Signature ModeOn / OffOffSwitches on auto-chop. On every step of the grid the plugin does one thing to your sound: plays it normally, silences it (gate), repeats a slice (stutter), plays it at half speed (half-time), slows it to a stop (tape-stop) or plays the last step backwards (reverse). It follows the host tempo (120 BPM if there is none). The window turns hot pink on black, with pink flashes on chop hits.

Signature Chop

ControlRange / optionsDefaultWhat it does
Intensity0 to 100 %100 %The chance that each step actually does its action. At 100% the pattern plays exactly as shown. Lower values leave more steps untouched, for a looser, subtler chop.
Chop Mix0 to 100 %100 %Mixes the chopped result with the normal sound. 100% = only the chopped sound. 50% = half and half, which makes the chops feel like accents on top of the groove.
Seed0 to 990A number from 0 to 99 that picks one 'version' of the randomness. The same seed always gives the same result, so you can find a variation you like and keep it. Roll seed picks a random one.
Rate1/8, 1/16, 1/321/16The length of each grid step. 1/8 = slow, big chops. 1/16 = standard. 1/32 = fast glitchy chops. A pattern is always 16 steps long.
PatternAuto, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, CustomAutoPicks the pattern of actions. Auto = a different built-in pattern each bar (the Seed changes which). 1-8 = one fixed built-in pattern. Custom = the steps you drew in the grid. Clicking a step in the grid switches this to Custom automatically.
Stutter Size1/2, 1/4, 1/81/4The size of the slice that repeats in a stutter step. 1/2 = two repeats per step, 1/4 = four, 1/8 = eight (fast, buzzy).
GateOn / OffOnTurn off to stop the chop from ever silencing a step. Steps that are switched off play normally.
StutterOn / OffOnTurn off to stop the chop from ever stuttering.
Half-timeOn / OffOnTurn off to stop the chop from ever slowing to half speed.
Tape-stopOn / OffOnTurn off to stop the chop from ever doing a tape-stop.
ReverseOn / OffOnTurn off to stop the chop from ever playing a step backwards.

4. Signature Mode (auto-chop)

Press @Reyaan. Signature Mode and the plugin starts auto-chopping your sound on the beat. The window also turns black and hot pink, with pink flashes on the chops (never more than about three a second).

The row of 16 boxes at the bottom is the pattern. Each box is one step, and each step does one of these things:

StepWhat happens
-Plays normally.
gateSilence for that step.
stutterRepeats a short slice of the step (the size is set by Stutter Size).
halfPlays the step at half speed - half-time, one octave down.
tapeA tape-stop: it slows down to a stop within the step.
reversePlays the previous step backwards.

Make it your own:

  • Click a step to change what it does (click again to move to the next kind; right-click goes backwards). The first time you click, Pattern switches to Custom and keeps whatever was showing, so you are editing from the current pattern. Your Custom pattern is saved with your project.
  • Clear steps sets all 16 to "normal" so you can build a pattern from nothing.
  • Intensity decides how many of the steps actually do their thing. Chop Mix blends the chopped result with the normal sound.
  • Rate sets the step length (1/8, 1/16 or 1/32). Stutter Size sets how long a stutter slice is.
  • Pattern chooses Auto, 1-8 or Custom. In Auto a different built-in pattern is picked every bar. Seed changes which patterns Auto picks and which steps Intensity skips; Roll seed picks a random seed. Same seed, same result.
  • Gate, Stutter, Half-time, Tape-stop, Reverse switches let you ban any kind of step from happening at all.

The playing step lights up. The chop follows your project's tempo (it assumes 120 BPM if the host does not send one) and works on the final sound, so it chops both your original and the reversed layer together. Use Time Align too and everything stays locked to the beat.

6. Themes

Only the black-and-pink look is built in. You (and anyone you share with) add the others yourselves. Click Theme:

  • Create a theme template writes a file called My Theme.json into the themes folder and shows it to you. The folder is %APPDATA%\Reyaan\Apprehension\Themes on Windows and ~/Library/Application Support/Reyaan/Apprehension/Themes on a Mac.
  • Open the file in Notepad (or TextEdit on a Mac - choose Format > Make Plain Text), change the colours (written like #ff3d9a), save it, then choose Reload themes. Your theme is now in the Theme menu. Send the .json file to a friend and they can drop it into the same folder.
  • Open themes folder opens the folder in Explorer (Windows) or Finder (Mac).

A theme can set the background gradient, glow colours, knob colours, panel colour and opacity, text colours, and a font name (any font installed on the computer). It can also point at a "image" (a still picture that slowly pans and zooms behind the plugin) or a "frames" folder of numbered pictures (for example exported from your own video with ffmpeg -i clip.mp4 -vf fps=24,scale=640:-1 frames/%04d.jpg) that plays as a loop. Only use pictures and footage you own or have permission to use.

The theme you choose is saved with your project.

7. Recipes for flipping samples

  1. Reverse riser into the drop: load Reverse Swell; raise Attack for a longer swell; put Mix at 100%. Automate Mix or Cutoff to build tension.
  2. Instant new loop from a chord sample: load Soul Chop, then try Seed values until the arrangement feels right. Use Chop Mix at 50% to keep the original groove under it.
  3. Re-key a sample to your beat: look at the Key Detector, decide which key you want, and turn Overall Pitch until the "After" key matches. It does not change the speed, so it stays in time.
  4. Slowed and lo-fi: load Vaporwave Slow or Lo-Fi Flip and lower Overall Pitch by 2-4 semitones. Use High Cut to darken it.
  5. Keep the groove clear: turn on Ducking in Input mode so the reversed layer drops while the original plays, or use Pump with 1/4 to make it breathe with the kick.
  6. Hold a moment: press Freeze on a nice moment; the last chunk loops backwards until you press it again. Add reverb and chorus for a pad.

8. Questions and troubleshooting

I hear nothing different. Check Mix is above 0%. The reverb, chorus, flangus, filters and ducking only act on the reversed sound, so they need some Mix. Also check Signature Mode and Time Align need the project to be playing.

Time Align does nothing / sounds late. It follows the host's beat position, so press play. If your DAW's delay compensation is switched off, the plugin's 25 ms of latency will not be corrected - turn it back on (FL Studio normally does this automatically unless you switched it off in its settings).

The sound is too quiet or too loud after a preset. Presets are level-matched approximately; use Output and, if needed, Soft Clip.

The reverb tail keeps going. That is the tail of the reverb (up to 15 s). Lower Decay or Mix.

The Key Detector says "listening...". It needs a few seconds of audio that is not too quiet. It reads your original (dry) sound, before any effect.

The text looks different on my computer. The plugin uses the first cursive font it finds on your computer (Apple Chancery or Snell Roundhand on a Mac; Monotype Corsiva, Palace Script, Edwardian Script, Brush Script, Segoe Script or Lucida Handwriting on a PC). A theme can name a different installed font.

Mac: macOS says the installer or plugin 'cannot be opened' or is 'from an unidentified developer'. This happens with any app or plugin that has not been signed with a paid Apple developer certificate. Right-click (or Control-click) the installer, choose Open, then Open again. If it was blocked already, open System Settings > Privacy & Security, scroll down and click Open Anyway. If your host still refuses to load the plugin, open Terminal and run xattr -cr /Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/VST3/Apprehension.vst3 (and the same for /Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/Components/Apprehension.component), then rescan plugins.

Mac: where are the plugins installed? The VST3 goes to /Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/VST3 and the Audio Unit (for Logic and GarageBand) to /Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/Components. The manual is also placed in /Library/Application Support/Reyaan/Apprehension. In FL Studio for Mac use Options > Manage plugins > Find installed plugins.

Pink flashing. Signature Mode flashes the background on some chop hits, at most about three times a second. If flashing bothers you, do not use Signature Mode, or ask for a calmer theme.

Where are my settings saved? Everything, including your Custom chop pattern, the preset name and the theme, is saved inside your project file.
